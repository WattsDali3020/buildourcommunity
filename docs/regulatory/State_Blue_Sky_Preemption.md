## 1. What Are “Blue Sky” Laws?

**Definition**: State-level securities laws that regulate the offer and sale of securities within each state. They generally require:
- Registration or qualification of securities offerings
- Registration of broker-dealers and agents operating in the state
- Anti-fraud provisions

Historically, issuers had to comply with the securities laws of every state in which they offered or sold securities (creating a “patchwork” of requirements).

---

## 2. Primary Sources

- **National Securities Markets Improvement Act of 1996 (NSMIA)** — This is the key federal law that created significant preemption of state blue sky laws for certain “covered securities.”
- **Securities Act of 1933** (as amended by NSMIA)
- SEC resources on Regulation A (which discuss preemption)

---

## 3. Federal Preemption Under NSMIA

NSMIA preempts state registration and qualification requirements for “covered securities.” Key categories of covered securities include:

- Securities listed on national exchanges (e.g., NYSE, Nasdaq)
- Securities offered under **Regulation A** (both Tier 1 and Tier 2 have preemption benefits, with Tier 2 having stronger preemption)
- Securities sold to “qualified purchasers” (in certain contexts)
- Securities issued by registered investment companies

**Important Distinction**:
- **Reg A+ Tier 2**: Strong federal preemption of state blue sky laws. Issuers generally do **not** need to register or qualify the offering in each state.
- **Reg A+ Tier 1** and **Reg D**: Much more limited preemption. Issuers often still need to make state notice filings or qualifications in states where they offer or sell securities.

---

## 4. Interaction with Reg A+ Tier 2 (Most Relevant to RevitaHub)

**Regulation A+ Tier 2** offers one of the best combinations for broader investor participation:
- Up to $75 million raise per 12 months
- Ability to accept non-accredited investors (with investment limits)
- **Federal preemption** of most state blue sky registration/qualification requirements

This is a major operational advantage compared to pure Reg D offerings, which often require state-by-state compliance work.

**RevitaHub Implication**:
- If the long-term goal is to enable everyday/community investors across multiple states (as aligned with the primary directive), **Reg A+ Tier 2** becomes significantly more attractive because of blue sky preemption.
- Early raises using Reg D may still require state notice filings, increasing complexity and cost as the number of states and investors grows.

---

## 5. Practical Considerations for Multi-State Offerings

Even with preemption, issuers may still need to:
- File Form D or other notices in certain states
- Comply with state anti-fraud rules
- Register broker-dealers and agents in states where they are operating (if applicable)
- Handle state-specific investor qualification or disclosure requirements in limited cases

**Platform Implication**: The compliance layer and backend systems should be designed to support multi-state notice filings and tracking, even if full qualification is preempted under Reg A+ Tier 2.
